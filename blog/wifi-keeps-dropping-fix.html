Why your shop's Wi-Fi keeps dropping — and easy fixes

Quick answer

Wi-Fi that keeps dropping is almost always one of three things: the router is in a bad spot, too many devices are fighting over it, or the router itself is old. Fixing the first two costs nothing. The third costs less than you'd think.

When the Wi-Fi drops mid-sale or mid-appointment, it's not just annoying — payments stall, music stops, and customers watch you reboot a box for the third time today. The good news: you usually don't need a technician or a new internet plan. You need to find which of a few common problems it is.

Here's how we diagnose it when we visit shops around Winnipeg, and what you can try yourself first.

Fix the easy stuff first

Check where your router sits. If it's inside a cabinet, behind a fridge, or jammed next to a microwave, the signal is being blocked or stepped on. Move it out in the open, up high, near the middle of the space. Then look at what else is connected. That smart speaker, the staff tablets, the card machine, three phones — they all share the same pipe. If your internet plan is old and small, that crowd is the problem.

Also: unplug the router, wait 30 seconds, plug it back in. It's a cliché because it works — old routers slowly get clogged and a restart clears them.

When it's the equipment

Routers wear out. If yours is more than about five years old, or was rented from your internet company and never upgraded, that's likely your culprit. A modern router handles many devices much better. For bigger spaces — a restaurant with a patio, a garage bay, a second floor — one box often can't reach everywhere. Extra "extender" units placed properly fill the gaps.

One more classic: the fridge, freezer, or old wiring on the same circuit causing power flickers. If the drop happens when something big kicks on, that's a clue.

Separate your customer Wi-Fi from yours

If customers use your Wi-Fi, set up a second, separate network for them. It keeps their phones from hogging the connection your card machine needs. Most modern routers can do this in the settings — it's called a guest network. It also protects your business devices from strangers being on them.
