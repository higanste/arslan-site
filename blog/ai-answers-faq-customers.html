Let AI answer your customers' same 10 questions

Quick answer

Write down the ten questions you hear every week. Teach a simple AI chat assistant the answers from your own words. Put it on your website. It answers those questions day and night — and hands anything complicated to you. Most owners get back an hour a day.

Ask yourself: how many times this week did you or your staff answer "Are you open on Sunday?" "Do you do walk-ins?" "How much for two rooms cleaned?" Same questions, different voices. Each one is small — until you add up a month of them.

What this assistant actually is

It's a chat box on your website, like the ones you've seen on bigger company sites. A visitor types a question in their own words, and it replies instantly, 24/7. It's not guessing: you feed it your real answers — your hours, your prices, your policies — so it speaks in your voice with your facts. Think of it as your best front-counter person, cloned for the repeat questions.

This is different from a rigid old "FAQ page," which nobody reads. The assistant lets customers ask naturally, the way they'd ask at the counter. The idea is a small, practical slice of what AI can actually do for a small business.

What it does with hard questions

A good setup knows its limits. If someone asks something the assistant wasn't given — a medical question, a custom quote, an upset customer — it says so and offers your phone number or a form, and flags the conversation for you. You see those in a simple list each morning and reply personally. Nothing goes out to a customer without your rules behind it.

That's the honest version of this technology — it's a triage desk, not a boss. The set-it-once idea is the same one we explain in automation, explained at your kitchen table.

Why it helps beyond saving time

Most people browse late at night or on Sundays when your counter is closed. If they get an instant answer at 10 p.m., they often book on the spot instead of forgetting about you. Every answered question is a customer you didn't lose to the next search result.

And it pairs well with the rest of your setup: your Google Maps listing brings people in, the assistant answers the questions, and your booking link closes the deal. One small loop, working while you sleep.
