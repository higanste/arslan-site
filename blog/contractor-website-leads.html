Contractors: getting calls from your website, not just word of mouth

Quick answer

A contractor website that brings calls needs just four things: photos of real finished jobs, a big phone number, a short list of what you do and where, and a quote request form. No blog, no fancy extras. Most contractor sites fail because they skip the photos and the phone number.

You've built your business on handshakes and referrals, and that's real. But the referrals are changing shape. These days a neighbour says "call Mike," and the homeowner's next move is to search Mike on their phone. If Mike has a rough site — or none — some of those calls never happen. That's money you already earned, leaking away.

The four things your site needs

Photos first. Before-and-after shots of decks, basements, bathrooms, roofs — real jobs, taken straight, in daylight. Ten good photos beat a thousand words. Next, your phone number big and tap-able at the top of every page; most trades customers would rather call than fill a form.

Third, a simple service list with the areas you cover. "Kitchen and bath renovations · Winnipeg, Headingley, St. Vital" beats vague slogans. Fourth, a short quote form — name, address, what they need, photos they can attach. People fill these at 10 p.m. when they can't call, and each one is a warm lead in your inbox by morning. A proper small-business website doesn't need more than this.

Get found in the right neighbourhoods

Your Google Business Profile matters as much as the site itself — it's what shows up when someone searches "fence repair near me." Keep the hours, phone, and service areas right, and let happy clients leave reviews. Ask at the final walkthrough, when they're happiest. Five honest reviews will do more than any ad.

Speed up the quote-and-invoice loop

Here's the part nobody tells you: the sale often dies in the follow-up. You send a quote Tuesday, the customer reads it Friday, and by then they've also called two other crews. Tools that send quotes quickly, track who's opened them, and turn approved quotes into invoices shorten that loop. Our guide on getting paid faster covers the simple options.

Do the four fixes, keep the reviews coming, and your website starts working like a quiet apprentice — out there quoting while you're on site.
