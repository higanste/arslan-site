"Automation" explained like you're standing in your kitchen

Quick answer

Automation just means: set something up once, and it happens by itself every time after that. Like a slow cooker or a thermostat. You do the setup, then the boring task runs itself while you do something only a human can do.

Forget the robot movies. You already use automation every day and never call it that. Your furnace kicks on when the house drops below a set temperature. Your coffee maker starts before you're out of bed. Your phone backs up photos overnight. That's the whole idea: a rule, a trigger, an action.

The three parts of any automation

Every automation has the same shape. A trigger â€” the thing that starts it. An action â€” what happens. And a rule that connects them. Business examples: When a customer books an appointment (trigger), send them a reminder text 24 hours before (action). When someone fills your quote form (trigger), drop it into your inbox with the details sorted (action). When an invoice sits unpaid for 7 days (trigger), send a polite nudge (action).

You're not teaching a computer to think. You're just writing down the chore list you repeat every day, once, and letting software repeat it for you.
