Printers are the worst — simple fixes for printer pain

Quick answer

Most printer problems come from three causes: the printer lost its connection to the Wi-Fi, the print queue is jammed with old jobs, or the ink is dry from sitting. All three have simple fixes you can do in minutes, without calling anyone.

There's an unwritten rule in every office and shop: the printer breaks at the worst moment. Customer waiting, invoice needed, and the machine says "offline" like it's on strike. We visit enough Winnipeg businesses to know this is close to universal. Here's the plain-English version of what's usually going on.

"Offline" usually means a lost connection

When your computer says the printer is offline, the printer and computer have simply lost touch. Nine times out of ten it's the Wi-Fi, not the printer. Restart the printer first — unplug it for 30 seconds. Then check whether it joined your Wi-Fi again. Many printers have a screen that shows the network name; if it's blank or wrong, reconnect it there.

If your Wi-Fi has been acting up generally, we wrote a separate guide on why shop Wi-Fi keeps dropping — fixing that often fixes the printer too.

Clear the stuck queue

Every print job gets in a line called a queue. When one job jams — say, a huge file with a weird font — everything behind it stops too, and nothing prints. On your computer, open the printer's settings and look for "see what's printing." Delete everything in the list, then print one test page. This alone fixes half the printer tantrums we see.

The ink dried out (again)

If pages come out with faint or striped colours, the ink may have dried — a common problem for businesses that only print occasionally, like a contractor who prints a few invoices a week. Run the printer's built-in "clean heads" or "nozzle check" from its own menu. And buy the right ink model; cheap lookalikes cause clogs faster.

When to stop fixing and start planning

If the machine is over five years old and eats more of your time than a part-time employee, do the math. A new basic printer costs less than a couple of repair calls. Also worth doing: printing labels and shipping slips in batches, which we cover in our paperwork guide — fewer print jobs, fewer jams.
