Email marketing rules in Canada (CASL) in plain English

Quick answer

CASL is Canada's anti-spam law. In short: only email people who agreed to hear from you, say who you are in every message, and give an easy way to unsubscribe. Do those three things and you're following the spirit of the law. This is general info, not legal advice.

You've probably heard the acronym CASL and assumed it was something for lawyers. It mostly comes down to basic courtesy, which you already practise in person. If you wouldn't corner someone at a trade show and pitch at them without asking, you already understand CASL.

The three simple rules

One: permission. Email people who actually said yes — they signed up, checked a box, or bought from you recently. Two: honesty. Every email must show your business name, your contact info, and a real address or P.O. box. Three: an easy out. Every message needs a working unsubscribe link, and if someone clicks it, stop emailing them within 10 business days.

That's the core. Yes, the actual law has more detail and big fines exist, but if you follow these three, you're doing what it asks. When in doubt, check the official Canadian government page on CASL or ask a lawyer — we're a tech shop, not one.

Who counts as "permission"?

Two flavours exist. Express consent: they clearly said yes, like signing up on a clipboard at your Winnipeg shop or checking a box online. Implied consent: they bought from you or asked about your services recently — that gives you a limited window to follow up. Customers who haven't bought or talked to you in two years generally need to say yes again.

So that list of emails from a trade show in 2019? Don't email it. Start fresh with a simple sign-up sheet by the register or on your website.

Using an email tool makes this easy

Don't send newsletters from your own inbox. Email tools like Mailchimp or similar handle the rules for you: they add your address automatically, they include an unsubscribe link, they keep the "do not email" list, and they send in small batches so you don't look like a spammer. They also give you the simple reports that show what people actually open.

Good email marketing is short and useful anyway — a monthly note, a seasonal offer, a heads-up about changed hours. That's why our own promise is the same shape: keep marketing small and sustainable, and never promise people spam. One email a month, that's it.
