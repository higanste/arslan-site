Backing up your files so a lost laptop isn't a disaster

Quick answer

A backup is a second copy of your files, kept somewhere safe. The simplest strong setup: turn on automatic cloud backup (files saved over the internet) on every computer that matters. Cost: a few dollars a month. Result: a stolen laptop costs you a laptop, not your business.

Picture the worst normal day: a laptop slides off a truck seat, or a coffee meets a keyboard, or the old desktop in the back office finally refuses to turn on. The machine is insured. The question is everything inside it — invoices, client lists, photos of jobs, that spreadsheet nobody else knows how to read.

The simple rule: two places minimum

One copy is zero copies, eventually. Hard drives die quietly, and laptops get left in cars. The safe pattern is two copies in two different places: your computer, plus a second copy that lives somewhere else entirely. That's it. You don't need to memorise any tech jargon beyond that.

The easiest "somewhere else" is the cloud — a word that just means someone else's secure computer you reach over the internet. Tools like Google Drive, OneDrive, or Dropbox can save your files there automatically, in the background, every time you change something. You set it once and forget it.

What to back up (it's less than you think)

You don't need to back up everything. Focus on what would hurt to lose: invoices and quotes, customer contact info, photos of work, contracts, your bookkeeping files, and anything only one person knows how to find. Most small shops can protect all of this with a shared drive folder where those files are simply kept from now on.

If some staff use personal phones for business photos, decide where those photos go too. And the passwords themselves should live in a password manager — covered in our password guide — so a lost phone doesn't take logins with it.

Test it before you need it

A backup you've never tested is a hope, not a plan. Once, on a quiet afternoon, open your backup on a different device and find last month's invoices. If you can see them, you're covered. If not, we can help you sort it in one visit.
