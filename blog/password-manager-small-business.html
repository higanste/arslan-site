One password everywhere? Safer (and easier) options

Quick answer

A password manager is an app that remembers all your passwords for you. You only ever memorise one strong password. It fills in the rest automatically — so every account can have a different, strong password without you doing extra work.

Most business owners we meet have the same setup: one password they like, reused everywhere, plus a sticky note for the ones that keep getting "too weak." It feels easier. Here's why it's risky, and what actually makes life easier instead.

Why one password everywhere is a problem

Companies get broken into all the time, and email lists get stolen in the process. If your one password is on that list, whoever has it tries it everywhere — your email, your supplier accounts, your bank. It's not personal; it's automated. One stolen password becomes many stolen accounts.

Your business email is the master key. Anyone inside it can reset your other passwords and pretend to be you. Protecting it first matters most — we cover the related scams in our guide to spotting fake emails.

What a password manager actually does

You install it on your phone and computer. The first time you log into any site, it offers to save that password. After that, it fills the box for you. When you need a new password, it creates a long random one — you never have to think it up. Everything is locked behind the one password you memorise, plus your fingerprint or phone code.

Good options for small teams cost a few dollars a month. Family-style plans also cover staff. It takes about an hour to set up your main accounts, and that hour removes a huge amount of risk.

Two habits that beat any software

First, turn on two-step sign-in (sometimes called two-factor or 2FA) on your email and your bank. It means a thief needs your password and your phone, which stops most takeovers by itself. Second, stop sharing passwords by text or sticky notes. If staff need access, most tools let you share a login safely inside the password manager.

These two steps, plus unique passwords, cover about 90% of what small businesses need. And if one laptop dies or gets stolen, nothing is lost except one password — which the thief can't use without your phone. For that kind of accident, our guide on backing up your files covers the rest.
