Staff email: why using your business name looks more pro

Quick answer

A business email — like marie@yourshopname.ca — costs a few dollars a month per person and instantly looks more professional than a personal Gmail. It also keeps your business messages in business hands: when a staff member leaves, the email stays with the company.

Here's a small test. You get two quotes in your inbox. One is from dave_the_guy78@gmail.com. One is from dave@mapleleafcontracting.ca. Same Dave, same work. Which one feels safer to send a deposit to? Most people feel the difference before they can explain it. Email addresses are tiny signals, but customers read them constantly.

What you actually need to buy

You need two things: a domain name — that's your business's own address on the internet, like yourshopname.ca, costing about $15–20 a year — and an email service that uses it, like Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, at a few dollars per person per month. You keep the same apps you already know (Gmail, Outlook); only the address changes.

Setup is a one-time job: register the domain, connect it to the email service, create each person's address. It's exactly the kind of thing our free checkup handles on the spot, and it pairs naturally with the advice in our website guide — same domain does both.

The part that matters more than looks

The bigger win is control. With personal Gmail accounts, your business lives in your staff's personal inboxes. When someone leaves, their emails — quotes, customer histories, supplier contacts — leave with them, and you can't get in. With business email, you own the accounts. You can forward a departing employee's mail to the next person with one setting.

It also helps you set up roles instead of people: info@ for general questions, quotes@ for estimates, billing@ for invoices. Those addresses stay stable even as staff change, and they make your automations easier — booking confirmations know exactly where to go.

One small warning

A nice domain doesn't stop scams. Crooks still send fake "invoice" emails hoping someone clicks. Train your team on the basics in our guide to spotting fake emails, and turn on two-step sign-in for every account.
