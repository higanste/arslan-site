Social media without it eating your whole week

Quick answer

Pick one platform. Take real photos as you work. Post a few times a month using a batch routine and a free scheduling tool. That's it. Consistency beats cleverness — and two hours a month is enough for most small shops.

Somewhere along the way, "be on social media" turned into a second job. Dance trends, daily stories, algorithms. Here's the good news for a working owner: you don't need any of that. You need to look alive and look trustworthy. That's a small, repeatable task — not a lifestyle.

Pick one platform and own it

Where do your customers actually hang out? Salons and restaurants live on Instagram. Trades and local services do well on Facebook, where neighbourhood groups are strong. Pick the one place and do it well instead of doing four places badly. An abandoned account from 2021 with one blurry photo sends a worse message than no account at all.

Make sure that profile links back to a real home base — a simple website and a tidy Google listing. Social media is rented land; your own pages are where the details live.

The 90-minute monthly routine

First, gather material as you go: a photo of a finished job, a full display case, a satisfied customer (with permission), a new product arriving. Phone photos are fine — good light matters more than good gear. Keep them in one folder on your phone.

Then, once or twice a month, sit down with a coffee and schedule the month. Free tools (like Meta Business Suite or Buffer) let you write posts now and set them to appear later, so you're not scrambling on a Tuesday. A simple rhythm works: one photo post a week, one short update (hours, holiday, new item), and one human post — a face, a thank-you, a story.

Let the tools do the repeating

AI can help here too — drafting captions from your notes, suggesting posting times — as long as you give it your real voice and photos. We cover the honest version of that in our no-hype AI guide. And keep your email list running quietly alongside it: a monthly note to subscribers, following the Canadian email rules, reaches people who actually asked to hear from you.

One last tip: reply to comments and messages within a day when you can. Slow replies undo fast posting. Then close the app and get back to work — the whole point of the routine is that it ends.
